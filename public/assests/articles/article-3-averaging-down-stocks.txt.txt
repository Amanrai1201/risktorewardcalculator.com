--- ARTICLE CONTENT ---

Averaging down—purchasing additional shares of a declining stock to lower the average entry price—is a common technique in equity investing. When applied systematically to high-quality assets, it lowers the required break-even point. When applied recklessly to deteriorating assets, it concentrates risk and accelerates losses.

AVERAGING DOWN DYNAMICS:
Initial Purchase: 100 Shares @ ₹100 --> Total Spend: ₹10,000
Stock Drops to: ₹60 (-40%)

SCENARIO A: Do Nothing
- New Price: ₹60
- Break-Even Needed: +66.7%
- Total Capital Risk: ₹10,000

SCENARIO B: Buy 100 More @ ₹60
- New Average Cost: ₹80
- Break-Even Needed: +33.3%
- Total Capital Risk: ₹16,000

The Mathematics of Weighted Average Price

The cost basis of a scaled position is governed by a weighted average calculation:
P_avg = Sum(P_i * Q_i) / Sum(Q_i)

Where:
- P_avg = Weighted Average Cost Basis
- P_i = Execution Price of tranches i
- Q_i = Quantity purchased in tranches i

Quantitative Comparison: Fixed Share vs. Fixed Capital Scaling

Assume an investor buys an initial tranche of 100 shares at ₹500 and the stock drops sequentially to ₹400 and ₹300.

Method 1: Fixed Share Quantity (100 Shares per Tranche)
- Tranche 1: 100 shares @ ₹500 = ₹50,000
- Tranche 2: 100 shares @ ₹400 = ₹40,000
- Tranche 3: 100 shares @ ₹300 = ₹30,000
P_avg = ₹120,000 / 300 = ₹400.00
To break even from ₹300, the stock must recover by +33.33%.

Method 2: Fixed Rupee Amount (₹50,000 per Tranche - Value Averaging)
- Tranche 1: 100.0 shares @ ₹500 = ₹50,000
- Tranche 2: 125.0 shares @ ₹400 = ₹50,000
- Tranche 3: 166.6 shares @ ₹300 = ₹50,000
P_avg = ₹150,000 / 391.6 = ₹383.04
To break even from ₹300, the stock must recover by +27.68%.

--- FAQ SECTION ---

Q: Why is averaging down dangerous for intraday traders?
A: Intraday trades rely on momentum and structural leverage. Averaging down on a declining intraday position violates risk management, increases position size as price moves against you, and often leads to margin calls or auto-square-off losses.

Q: What is the difference between Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) and averaging down?
A: Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) is a scheduled, asset-agnostic investment plan executed over time regardless of market direction. Averaging down is a reactive decision to buy additional shares specifically because an asset's price has declined.
