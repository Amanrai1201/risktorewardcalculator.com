--- ARTICLE CONTENT ---

The 1% Risk Rule is a fundamental pillar of professional capital preservation. It dictates that a trader should never risk more than 1% of their total account equity on a single trade setup.

Many beginner traders confuse Position Size with Capital at Risk, leading to improper trade allocation.

THE 1% RULE IN PRACTICE:
Total Account Capital : ₹5,00,000
Maximum Allowed Risk  : 1% = ₹5,000 (Maximum loss if Stop Loss is hit)

INCORRECT APPROACH: Buy ₹5,000 worth of stock total
CORRECT APPROACH  : Calculate share quantity so that (Entry - SL) * Qty plus execution fees = ₹5,000

The Position Sizing Formula

To apply the 1% rule, derive share quantity based on stop-loss distance rather than fixed cash allocation:
Account Risk (₹) = Total Capital * 0.01
Risk Per Share (₹) = Entry Price - Stop Loss Price
Max Position Size (Shares) = Account Risk (₹) / (Risk Per Share (₹) + Per-Share Charges)

Mathematical Worked Example:
- Account Capital: ₹2,00,000
- Maximum Risk (1%): ₹2,00,000 * 0.01 = ₹2,000
- Stock Entry Price: ₹250
- Stop Loss Price: ₹240
- Risk Per Share (Price Distance): ₹10
- Raw Quantity = ₹2,000 / ₹10 = 200 Shares

Total Position Capital Value: 200 * ₹250 = ₹50,000 (25% of total account capital)
Maximum Monetary Risk if Stopped Out: 200 * ₹10 = ₹2,000 (Exactly 1% of account capital)

Mathematical Survival Proof: 1% Rule vs. 5% Fixed Allocation (10 Losing Trades from ₹1,00,000):
- Capital (1% Fixed Risk): Starts ₹1,00,000 -> Trade 10: ₹90,438 (-9.56% drawdown)
- Capital (5% Fixed Risk): Starts ₹1,00,000 -> Trade 10: ₹59,873 (-40.13% drawdown)
- Capital (Unmanaged 10% Risk): Starts ₹1,00,000 -> Trade 10: ₹34,868 (-65.13% drawdown)

--- FAQ SECTION ---

Q: Can aggressive accounts use a 2% or 3% risk rule?
A: Yes. Experienced traders with proven positive statistical expectancy sometimes risk 2% per trade. However, risking 3% or more significantly increases drawdown risk during market anomalies, requiring higher win rates to recover capital.

Q: How does portfolio leverage affect the 1% risk rule?
A: Leverage increases total buying power, but the 1% risk rule must always be calculated against your underlying account equity, not leveraged buying limits.
